Most players lose their first dozen games to the same misunderstanding: they count *completed pattern lines* and assume that is the score. It is not. Where a tile lands on the wall matters far more than how many tiles you landed.

## A settled tile scores the runs it joins

When a completed pattern line settles one tile onto your wall, that tile scores:

- **1**, if it sits alone with no settled neighbours.
- Otherwise, **the length of every unbroken run it joins** — horizontal, vertical, or both, counted separately and added together.

So a tile that extends a horizontal run of three *and* a vertical run of two scores 4 + 3 = 7. The same tile dropped in an empty corner of the wall scores 1.

This single rule is the whole game. Three completed lines settled into three separate corners score 1 + 1 + 1 = 3. Two completed lines stacked in one column score 1 + 2 = 3 — the same points from a third less drafting, and with a column bonus now in reach.

> Drafting fast is not the game. Drafting so that each tile lands beside the last one is the game.

## The floor line

Every tile on your floor line at the end of a round costs you points, and the floor is cleared afterwards. The "1" marker occupies a floor slot too, so leading the next round is never free.

The practical rule: compare the penalty against what the overflow buys you. Taking three tiles for a two-slot line to complete a line that will score 5 is a clear profit. Taking three tiles for a line that will not settle this round, and eating the penalty for nothing, is how a good position becomes a bad one.

## End-game bonuses

The game ends when either player completes a full horizontal row. Then:

| Bonus | Worth |
| --- | --- |
| Each complete horizontal row | +2 |
| Each complete vertical column | +7 |
| Each color you hold all five of | +10 |

The asymmetry is deliberate and it should drive your endgame. A row is what *ends* the game; a column is what *wins* it. One column is worth more than three completed rows, and a full color set is worth more than that again.

## What your daily score actually is

On the [Daily Challenge](/daily), the number that goes on the board is not your final score — it is your **margin**: your final score minus your opponent's. Ties are broken by elapsed time, and then by who submitted first.

Margin rather than raw score is what makes the boards comparable. A 62-point game against an opponent who scored 60 is a closer thing than a 45-point game against an opponent who scored 20, and the board should say so. It also means a defensive move that costs you 3 points and costs the opponent 9 is a good move, which is true and which raw score would hide.

Because each of the six opponents plays to a different standard, each has [its own board](/leaderboard). A margin against Extreme and a margin against Easy are not the same achievement, so they are never mixed.
