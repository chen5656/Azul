Most players lose their first dozen games to the same misunderstanding: they count *completed context lines* and assume that is the score. It is not. Where a token crystallizes on the **memory grid** matters far more than how many raw tokens you gathered.

## A crystallized token scores the memory clusters it joins

When a completed context line settles one token onto your memory grid, that token scores:

- **1**, if it sits alone with no adjacent memory nodes.
- Otherwise, **the length of every unbroken cluster it joins** — horizontal, vertical, or both, counted separately and added together.

So a token that extends a horizontal sequence of three *and* a vertical sequence of two scores 4 + 3 = 7. The same token dropped in an isolated corner of your memory grid scores 1.

This single rule is the whole game. Three completed lines settled into three separate corners score 1 + 1 + 1 = 3. Two completed lines stacked in one column score 1 + 2 = 3 — the same points from a third less drafting, and with a permanent column bonus now in reach.

> Gathering tokens quickly is not the game. Directing your attention so that each token crystallizes adjacent to existing memory nodes is the game.

## The hallucination line

Every token on your hallucination line at the end of a round costs you points, and the hallucination queue is cleared afterwards. The initiative ("1") marker occupies a hallucination slot too, so leading the next round is never free.

The practical rule: compare the hallucination penalty against what the overflow buys you. Taking three tokens for a two-slot line to complete a context line that will immediately score 5 points is a clear profit. Taking three tokens for a line that will not settle this round, and eating the hallucination penalty for nothing, is how a good consciousness state degrades.

## End-game convergence bonuses

The game ends when either the Human or the AI completes a full horizontal row in memory. Then:

| Bonus | Worth |
| --- | --- |
| Each complete horizontal row | +2 |
| Each complete vertical column | +7 |
| Each color frequency you hold all five of | +10 |

The asymmetry is deliberate and it should drive your endgame. A completed row is what *triggers convergence* (ends the game); a completed column is what *wins* it. One column bonus is worth more than three completed rows, and a full color set is worth more than that again.

## What your daily score actually is

On the [Daily Challenge](/daily), the number that goes on the leaderboard is not your raw score — it is your **margin**: your final score minus your AI opponent's. Ties are broken by elapsed time, and then by submission timestamp.

Margin rather than raw score is what reflects true tactical dominance. A 62-point game against an AI that scored 60 is a close call; a 45-point game against an AI you forced down to 20 via hallucination overloads is a commanding victory. It also means an aggressive defensive move that costs you 3 points of hallucination while forcing 9 points of hallucination onto the AI is a great move.

Because each of the six AI opponents operates on a different neural architecture, each has [its own leaderboard](/leaderboard). A margin against Extreme and a margin against Easy are distinct achievements, so they are never mixed.
